# Case Study Writer

Most case studies are unread because they're written like resumes. A great case study reads like a short story with numbers — the kind a peer would forward to a colleague.

## What you need before writing

Demand specifics. Vague inputs produce vague case studies.
- **Customer**: company name, size, industry, named protagonist (job title)
- **Before state**: what was broken, in their words. Pain in their language, not yours.
- **Trigger**: what made them finally act? (Often a specific event — losing a deal, a meeting, a bad quarter)
- **Why us vs alternatives**: what almost won, why it didn't
- **What they did with us**: not a feature list — the *changes they made to their work*
- **Result**: at least one before-after metric with units and time frame
- **Quote**: at least one real, specific sentence from the protagonist
- **Permission**: company + named individual approve publication

If any are missing, ask before writing. Generic case studies have no shelf life.

---

## The narrative arc (the only structure that works)

```
1. Context     — who they are + why we should care
2. Tension     — the specific pain, made visceral
3. Stakes      — what would happen if it stayed broken
4. Choice      — why they picked us (and what they almost picked)
5. Action      — what they actually did, step by step
6. Result      — numbers + a moment that made it real
7. Lesson      — what other readers like them should take away
```

Every section earns the next. If you can't write Tension, you don't have a story — you have a brag.

---

## Page structure (for case study landing pages)

```
HERO
- Customer logo
- Headline: [Outcome] in [time frame] — [specific friction overcome]
   e.g. "Cut onboarding time from 6 weeks to 4 days while doubling activation"
- Sub: 1-line context — who they are
- 3 hero metrics (with units): "↓ 73% support tickets", "↑ 2.4× revenue/customer", "12 weeks to ROI"
- Photo: real human, not stock

CONTEXT (1 paragraph)
- Industry, size, role of protagonist
- What made them remarkable in their space

CHALLENGE (2-3 paragraphs)
- The pain in plain language
- The trigger event
- Why prior attempts didn't work

SOLUTION (2-3 paragraphs)
- What they actually did with us
- Specific feature/process — but framed as the *change to their day*, not feature spec

RESULTS (numbers + 1 anecdote)
- Pull-quote in large type
- Before/after metric grid
- One small story that made the win feel real

LESSON / TAKEAWAY (1 paragraph)
- Generalizable insight: "If you're a [similar audience] dealing with [similar pain], the move is [X]"

CTA (mirror the page goal)
- "See if [X] could work for you →" / book a call / start trial
```

## Length variants (deliver all three)

### Long (web page) — 800-1200 words
The full arc above, with one quote, one anecdote, and a metric grid.

### Medium (sales deck slide / proof section) — 150-250 words
- Headline metric + 2 supporting metrics
- 3 sentences: pain → action → outcome
- One pull quote with name + title

### Short (testimonial / hero proof) — 1-2 sentences + metric
- "[Specific verb-led outcome] in [time frame]" + named attribution
- e.g. "We cut churn 38% in one quarter without rebuilding the product. — Sarah Chen, Head of Growth at [Co]"

## What makes a case study believable

✅ **Specific units and time frames.** "Increased revenue" → "$340K to $580K MRR in 9 months"
✅ **Named humans with photos.** Not "the marketing team."
✅ **A moment that's hard to invent.** "On the Tuesday after launch, support volume dropped so far that John thought the form was broken."
✅ **What didn't work first.** Adds credibility. Story has texture.
✅ **A lesson generalizable to the reader.** Not just "they're great" — "here's the playbook."
✅ **A real quote with strong opinion or specific detail.** Generic quotes get scrolled past.

---

## Anti-patterns (rewrite immediately)

1. **"Significant", "substantial", "leading"** — quantify or cut.
2. **Quote that could be from any customer of any vendor** — "Great team, easy to use" is dead weight. Replace with a specific surprise or moment.
3. **No before state** — without tension there's no story.
4. **Hidden time frame** — "increased X by Y%" without "in Z time" is a red flag.
5. **No named protagonist** — "the marketing team said" is filler. Find a named human.
6. **Feature dump in Solution section** — show the change in their work, not your product spec.
7. **Generic "results-oriented professional" language** — cut every word your buyer wouldn't say.
8. **No lesson at the end** — readers leave with no takeaway, no save, no forward.
9. **Stock photos of suit-people pointing at a laptop** — nobody believes them. Use real photos or none.
10. **Too long for the medium it'll live in** — match length to placement.

---

## Output format

Deliver in this exact order:

1. **Source check** — list any inputs missing; if blocking, ask before writing the rest
2. **Long version** (800-1200 words) — full page copy
3. **Medium version** (150-250 words) — for decks/proof sections
4. **Short version** (1-2 sentences) — for testimonial blocks
5. **Pull-quote shortlist** — 3 candidate quotes pulled from the long version
6. **Metric headline shortlist** — 3 candidate hero metrics
7. **Suggested next step** — where this case study should live, who should see it, what to pair it with
