# Competitor Analysis

Most competitor research is feature checklists — useless. The point is finding the unclaimed positioning where the buyer is underserved AND we can credibly win.

## Inputs you need first

- **Our product/offer** in one sentence
- **Target buyer** (one specific persona)
- **The buyer's job-to-be-done** (what they're hiring something to do)
- **Geography** (matters more than people admit)
- **5 competitors** — if user gives fewer, ask. If they say "you pick", use:
  - 2 direct competitors (same buyer, similar offer)
  - 1 adjacent (same buyer, different angle)
  - 1 lower-end (cheaper, "good enough" alternative)
  - 1 status-quo / DIY (what they do today instead of buying anything)

The status-quo competitor is the one most people skip — and it usually wins more deals than any vendor.

---

## The 8 axes (score each competitor 1-5)

| Axis | What you're looking at |
|---|---|
| **Target buyer** | Who they actually serve (look at testimonials, case studies, pricing tier) |
| **Core promise** | The single outcome they sell (one sentence pulled from their hero) |
| **Price + model** | How they charge — flat, usage, seats, retainer, freemium — and the entry price |
| **Proof** | What evidence they show — logos, numbers, case studies, reviews, awards |
| **Distribution** | How buyers find them — SEO, paid, partnerships, content, sales-led |
| **Brand voice** | Formal/casual/edgy/safe; specific words they use repeatedly |
| **UX / product feel** | Onboarding speed, dashboard design, support quality, perceived premium |
| **Fatal weakness** | The complaint that shows up repeatedly in reviews / Reddit / Twitter |

For each, score 1 (weak) → 5 (dominant) AND write 1 line of evidence ("Hero says X", "Pricing page is Y").

---

## The positioning map

After scoring, plot competitors on **two axes the buyer actually cares about**. Pick the axes from the buyer's POV, not yours.

Common axis pairs (pick one):
- Price ↔ Premium feel
- Speed-to-value ↔ Power
- DIY ↔ Done-for-you
- Generalist ↔ Specialist
- Casual ↔ Enterprise

Place each competitor as a dot. Empty quadrants = positioning gaps.

## How to actually research (not just read homepages)

For each competitor, look at:
1. **Hero + pricing pages** — they spent the most time here
2. **2 case studies** — reveals their actual buyer
3. **Their about page** — reveals voice and origin story
4. **G2 / Capterra / Reddit reviews** — fatal weakness lives here
5. **Their last 3 LinkedIn / Twitter posts** — current marketing focus
6. **Their job listings** — where they're investing tells you more than press releases
7. **Their founder's recent talks/podcasts** — strategic intent

For status-quo competitor, ask:
- What spreadsheet are they using today?
- What free tool do they hack together?
- What internal process do they keep instead of buying?

---

## Anti-patterns

1. **Feature comparison checklist** — lists every checkbox; misses the actual war
2. **Only direct competitors** — ignores the status quo, which is the real enemy
3. **Scoring on axes WE care about** — score on axes the BUYER cares about
4. **Recommending we beat them at their game** — pick a different game instead
5. **No primary research** — reading their homepage is necessary, not sufficient
6. **Conclusion = "we should do all the things"** — pick ONE gap; specificity wins
7. **Treating bigger competitors as unbeatable** — their size IS the weakness in the right gap
8. **Forgetting time-of-write date** — competitive positions decay; date the doc
